Staying Healthy

The screening tests and vaccines we recommend for most adults, based on New Brunswick’s programs and national guidelines.

This page is a general guide for adults at average risk. If you have symptoms, a strong family history, or an ongoing health condition, your physician may recommend something different. Please don’t book a visit just to go over this list. Bring it up at your next appointment.

We follow a careful approach to preventive care. We recommend a test when good evidence shows it does more good than harm. We don’t recommend tests that often lead to false alarms, extra procedures or worry without helping people live longer or better.

Cancer screening

Cervical cancer (Pap test)

  • Who: women and anyone with a cervix, aged 25 to 69, who has ever been sexually active.
  • When to start: at age 25, or 3 years after first sexual contact if that comes later.
  • How often: every 3 years once you have had three normal Pap tests in a row.
  • When to stop: after age 69, if your tests over the past 10 years were normal.
  • How: book a Pap test with your family physician, or with one of our nurses (see Additional Services). New Brunswick is moving to HPV testing over the coming years, and we will update this page when that changes.

Breast cancer (mammogram)

  • Ages 50 to 74: a screening mammogram every 2 to 3 years.
  • Ages 40 to 49: screening is your choice. Mammograms at this age find some cancers early, but they also lead to more false alarms and extra tests. We’re happy to talk this through with you.
  • How: in New Brunswick, people aged 40 to 74 with no symptoms can book a screening mammogram themselves, without a referral. Call the cancer screening information line at 1-844-777-3443 to find a screening site.

Colon cancer (at-home stool test)

  • Who: adults aged 50 to 74 at average risk.
  • How often: every 2 years.
  • How: the NB Colon Cancer Screening Program mails an invitation. You can also request the at-home test (FIT) by calling 1-844-777-3443.
  • If a parent, brother, sister or child has had colon cancer, tell your physician. You may need a colonoscopy instead.

Lung cancer

  • Who: adults aged 50 to 74 who smoke now or used to smoke, with a smoking history of 20 years or more.
  • How: a low-dose CT scan through New Brunswick’s lung screening program. Ask your physician whether you qualify.

Prostate cancer (PSA test)

Routine PSA blood tests are not recommended for men without symptoms. Most cancers the test finds would never have caused harm, and follow-up biopsies and treatment carry real risks. If you have questions or a family history, talk with your physician.

Other checks

  • Blood pressure: checked during regular visits. Many pharmacies also have a free machine you can use.
  • Diabetes: your physician will consider your risk (age, weight, family history, past high sugars) and order a blood test every 3 to 5 years if you are at higher risk.
  • Cholesterol: a blood test about every 5 years from age 40, or earlier if you have risk factors such as diabetes, smoking or a strong family history of heart disease.
  • Abdominal aortic aneurysm: men aged 65 to 80 should have a one-time ultrasound of the aorta, the main blood vessel in the abdomen.
  • Bone health (osteoporosis): women 65 and older should have their fracture risk assessed. Your physician will order a bone density scan if it is needed. If you are younger, or a man with risk factors (a previous fracture, long-term steroid use), ask your physician.
  • Chlamydia and gonorrhea: if you are under 30 and sexually active, a yearly urine test is recommended. The test is easy to add at any visit.

Vaccines for adults

Most of these vaccines are free in New Brunswick. Pharmacists give many of them, and our nurses can also help (call 506-645-8888 to ask).

  • Flu (influenza): every fall, for everyone 6 months and older. Free.
  • Tetanus and diphtheria (Td): a booster every 10 years. One of these boosters, as an adult, should include whooping cough (Tdap). Free.
  • Pregnancy: a whooping cough vaccine (Tdap) during every pregnancy protects the newborn. Free.
  • Pneumonia (pneumococcal): one dose at age 65 or older. Free.
  • RSV: one dose for adults 75 and older, and Indigenous adults 60 and older. Free.
  • Shingles (Shingrix): recommended for adults 50 and older, as two doses 2 to 6 months apart. It is not publicly funded in New Brunswick. Check whether your drug plan covers it.
  • HPV: given in school in grade 7, and free for anyone under 27 who missed it.
  • Measles, mumps and rubella (MMR): if you were born in 1970 or later and aren’t sure you had two doses, ask us.
  • Travelling? Some destinations need extra vaccines. Book about 6 to 8 weeks before you leave.

Everyday health

  • Smoking and vaping: quitting is the single biggest thing you can do for your health. Ask your physician, nurse or pharmacist about help to quit.
  • Activity: aim for 150 minutes of moderate activity a week, such as brisk walking. Balance and strength exercises help prevent falls as you get older.
  • Alcohol: less is better for your health.

Keep track

Knowing when you last had each test or vaccine helps us help you. See Before You Visit for what to bring to your appointments.
