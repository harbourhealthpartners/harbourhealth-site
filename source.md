## ADDITIONAL SERVICES

Heading: "Additional Services"

**Cervical Screening Clinics**
Our nurses are trained to do routine cervical screening. You can have it done by one of our nurses or by your own family physician. Call the office and tell us who you would prefer to see.

New Brunswick now screens with an HPV test (sample collected the same way as a Pap test). For most people with normal results:
- Start at 25 if you have a cervix and have ever been sexually active (any genital contact, with partners of any gender).
- Every 5 years if your HPV test is negative.
- Stop after 69 if your screening over the past 10 years was normal.
- Still needed if pregnant, past menopause, HPV-vaccinated, or after a hysterectomy that left the cervix in place.
- Can stop after a total hysterectomy for a reason other than cancer, with no history of abnormal cervical cells or HPV.

If a result is not normal, your physician will explain what happens next.

Higher risk (talk to your physician):
- Weakened immune system (organ transplant, HIV, immune-suppressing medicines): every 3 years instead of 5.
- Past high-grade abnormal cells or cervical cancer: follow the specialist's plan.
- Symptoms at any age (unusual bleeding, pain during sex, unusual or ongoing discharge): needs assessment, not screening. Book with your family physician.

Please note: nursing visits are for routine screening only. If you have symptoms or concerns, please book with your family physician.

Source: NB Cervical Cancer Screening Program, Clinical Practice Guidelines for HPV Primary Screening (NB Cancer Network, 2026): https://www.gnb.ca/content/dam/GNB3/t/hw-sme/mhd/cancer/tools-outils/docs/clinical-practice-guidelines

**Diabetes Educator Clinics**
To help guide diabetic patients in our practice with the management of their diabetes, we have the services of two Diabetes Educators, a specially trained dietitian and nurse, on Fridays at our clinic. Visits are by appointment only. Ask your family physician if this would be appropriate for you.

**Vaccinations**
Our nurses provide all vaccine care at the clinic. We stock all routine childhood and adult vaccines, apart from those given in school and the COVID-19 vaccine, which is available at pharmacies. If you would like to know which vaccines are recommended for you, ask to speak with one of our nurses. Links to Staying Healthy for the adult schedule.

**Other Services**
Our nurses can help you with other services without needing to see your family physician. Examples include ear syringing, routine injections (Vitamin B12, DepoProvera) and dressing changes. Contact our office and ask to book a nursing appointment for one of these reasons.

## BEFORE YOU VISIT

Heading: "What to know before you visit"

**Meet and Greet**
If you're a new patient, welcome. We're excited to get to know you. Most physicians will usually have a longer "Meet and Greet" appointment during your first visit, where they get to know you and your health history. They may deal with active issues at this appointment, or ask you to book again soon to discuss current concerns.

**What do I need to bring?**
Bring all your current prescription medications and your up-to-date Medicare card. If you're a new patient, you may also fill out and bring a New Patient Questionnaire, or complete one in our waiting room on arrival.

It is also helpful if we know:
- Family history: whether your first-degree relatives (parents, siblings, children) have had heart disease, diabetes, strokes, or cancers, and at what age they were diagnosed.
- Medications: an up-to-date list, or your medications in their packages.
- Surgeries, hospitalizations, or illnesses: the year, and for surgeries which surgeon operated. (Example: diagnosed with hypertension in 2010.)
- Screening tests: when your last cervical screening (Pap or HPV test), mammogram, diabetes, cholesterol, bone density, and colon cancer (stool testing) screens were.
- Immunizations: when your last tetanus, flu, or pneumonia shot was.

Links: Contact Info → Contact page; Our Policies → Policies page.

## SCHEDULE & URGENCIES

Heading: "Schedule and Urgencies"

**Our Schedule**
Our clinic is open 5 days per week, with daytime availability for urgent needs. If you are experiencing an emergency, always call 911 or go to the local Emergency Department.

**Refills**
If you are running low on a medication, our same-day urgent appointments allow a physician to give you a short supply, but will encourage you to make an appointment with your regular physician for a full refill. No narcotic, benzodiazepine, or stimulant medications will be refilled during these appointments. We book an appointment for all refill requests to reassess the condition.

**Which physician is working?**
Our schedule changes each week. All physicians in our group have access to your electronic medical record to give you the best care possible. Please call your own physician first; their staff or voicemail will direct you to the appropriate clinic if they are not available to see you themselves.

## FAQ

Heading: "Frequently Asked Questions"

**What are the hours?**
- Monday to Thursday: 8:00 AM to 5:00 PM
- Friday: 8:00 AM to 4:00 PM

**What if I need care when the clinic is closed?**
In an emergency, call 911 or go to the nearest Emergency Department. Otherwise:
- Call 811 (Tele-Care) any time, day or night, to speak with a nurse about your symptoms and where to go.
- Virtual Care NB: virtual appointments for common, non-urgent problems, 8 a.m. to 8 p.m., seven days a week. Start with the online symptom checker or by calling 811. https://www.gnb.ca/en/topic/health-wellness/access-health-care/virtual-care.html
- Ask a pharmacist: NB pharmacists can assess and treat many common ailments and renew many prescriptions, at no charge. https://www.gnb.ca/en/topic/health-wellness/access-health-care/pharmacy-services.html
- Go to a local walk-in clinic.
- Book an appointment online (https://nb001.myaccession.com/booking/) if it can wait until your physician is available.

**Where can I get help for mental health or a crisis?**
If you or someone else is in immediate danger, call 911.
- 988 Suicide Crisis Helpline: call or text 988, 24 hours a day.
- NB Addiction and Mental Health Helpline: 1-866-355-5550, 24 hours a day.
- Mobile Crisis Services (Saint John area): 1-888-811-3664.
- Community addiction and mental health services: https://www.gnb.ca/en/topic/health-wellness/mental-health/mental-services.html
You can also book with your family physician to talk about your mental health.

**What do I do if I am outside the province or country?**
Research the local health-care system before visiting any other province or country so you are familiar with the options available. For non-urgent care you can also call your clinic or book an appointment online. You may wait until you can be seen by your physician, or your physician may be able to speak with you by phone.

**I want a physician in your clinic. Where do I sign up?**
We are currently not accepting new patients. New patients are added through NB Health Link. To register, visit https://nbhealthlink.ca/pages/registration/.

**Is my medical information safe?**
Yes. Your medical history is kept in an EMR that is compliant with New Brunswick's Personal Health Information Privacy and Access Act (PHIPAA). Explain in plain language what PHIPAA means: information used only for your care or as the law permits; held securely with encryption and controlled access; only your circle of care may look at it and access is logged; not shared outside that circle without consent except where law requires; you may see your record, request a correction, and ask who accessed it.

**Does my physician use an AI scribe?**
Some physicians use Heidi, an AI scribe, which listens to the visit and drafts the clinical note. Heidi is PHIPAA compliant. The physician reviews and edits every note before it enters the chart and remains responsible for it. Benefits: more eye contact and attention during the visit, more complete and accurate notes, less after-hours paperwork so more appointment time. Patients may ask their physician not to use it.

**Have a question that isn't listed?** Contact us.

## STAYING HEALTHY (health-maintenance.html)

Heading: "Staying Healthy". Lead: "The screening tests and vaccines we recommend for most adults, based on New Brunswick's programs and national guidelines."

Conservative, average-risk adult guide. The HTML page holds the full wording; keep this outline in step.
- Note: general guide for average risk; symptoms, strong family history or ongoing conditions may change advice; bring it up at your next appointment rather than booking just for this.
- Cancer screening: cervical (HPV test, 25 to 69, every 5 years if negative, every 3 years if immunosuppressed; links to Additional Services); breast (50 to 74 every 2 to 3 years; 40 to 49 is a choice to discuss; NB self-referral 40 to 74, 1-844-777-3443); colon (FIT every 2 years, 50 to 74; family history → tell physician); lung (you might qualify if 50 to 74, current or former smoker, 20+ years smoking; discuss with your family physician); prostate (routine PSA not recommended).
- Other checks: blood pressure; diabetes risk-based testing every 3 to 5 years; cholesterol about every 5 years from 40; one-time AAA ultrasound for men 65 to 80; fracture-risk assessment for women 65+; yearly chlamydia/gonorrhea test under 30 if sexually active.
- Vaccines: nurses provide all vaccine care; clinic stocks all routine childhood and adult vaccines except school-program vaccines and COVID-19; nurses are the contact for reviewing which vaccines are right for you. Every year: flu, COVID-19 (free, at pharmacies). Other: Td every 10 years with one adult Tdap; Tdap each pregnancy; pneumococcal once at 65+; RSV once at 75+ (Indigenous 60+); Shingrix 50+ (two doses, not publicly funded); HPV grade 7 and free under 27; MMR check if born 1970 or later; travel vaccines 6 to 8 weeks ahead.
- Everyday health: quit smoking/vaping help, 150 min/week activity plus balance and strength, less alcohol.
- Sources: NB cervical, breast and colon programs, NB HPV primary screening guidelines (2026), NB Immunization Schedule, Canadian Task Force on Preventive Health Care (replaced June 2026 by the National Advisory Committee on Preventive Health Services; its guidelines remain the latest national ones), Canadian Cardiovascular Society, Canadian Immunization Guide (NACI), Choosing Wisely Canada.
- Hepatitis C screening deliberately not included.
